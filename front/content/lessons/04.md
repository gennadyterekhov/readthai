---
title: Vowel carrier
---

Thai alphabet is an [abugida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abugida),
it means vowels are attached to consonants and cannot stand independently.  
So, if we want to write just /aː/ without a consonant, we need to use the so-called vowel carrier.

<br>

• อ /ɔː/ or vowel carrier <PlayPhoneme p="ɔ"></PlayPhoneme>   

<br>

##### with vowel carrier:

<Word w="อา"></Word> `/aː/`   
<Word w="อาน"></Word> `/aːn/` - saddle

<br>
<br>

##### with อ as a vowel `/ɔː/` :

<Word w="กอ"></Word> `/kɔː/` - _classifier_   
<Word w="กอน"></Word> `/kɔːn/` - child   
<Word w="กอก"></Word> `/kɔːk/` - olive  
  
<Word w="นอ"></Word> `/nɔː/` - horn   
<Word w="นอน"></Word> `/nɔːn/` - to sleep   
<Word w="นอก"></Word> `/nɔːk/` - outside  

<Word w="รอ"></Word> `/rɔː/` - to wait   
<Word w="รอน"></Word> `/rɔːn/` - to reduce     
<Word w="รอก"></Word> `/rɔːk/` - pulley   

<br>
<br>

##### both a vowel carrier and a vowel


<Word w="ออ"></Word> `/ɔː/` - to crowd  
<Word w="ออก"></Word> `/ɔːk/` - to pay, out
